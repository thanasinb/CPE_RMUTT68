## 2. How the program is judged
- **Evidence over description.** PEVs "triangulate": SER ↔ lecturers ↔ students ↔ stakeholders ↔ what they see. A good write-up without matching evidence fails. They ask "show me", "how do students see this?", and "what did you do when results missed the target?"
- **The OBE chain must be closed:** PEO → PO → PI → CLO → teaching → assessment → attainment → CQI → change → result after the change.
- **PEVs weight criteria 2, 3, 4 and 5 most, especially criterion 4 (continuous improvement) and the capstone in criterion 5.**
- **Finding levels:**
  - **Deficiency:** not compliant; must be fixed.
  - **Weakness:** partly non-compliant and affects outcome attainment.
  - **Concern:** compliant now but at risk.
  - **Remark:** optional suggestion.
  - Findings are written as: criterion → evidence → impact.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Full accreditation: 6 years, interim report at 3 years.
  - With requirements: at most 3 years.
  - Provisional: for a new program with no graduates; re-apply after 2 graduating classes.
  - Defer: 1 year.
  - Decline/revoke: re-apply after at least 2 years.
  - **"No PO assessment, or POs that cannot be confirmed from Course Portfolios" is grounds for both defer and decline.**
- **Five-level consistency check.** At Level 1 the secretariat screens the SER before accepting it: 4-year program, 120–150 credits, 2 years of data, PO assessment present.

## 3. Eligibility and timeline (Manual §8 and the schedule)
- 4-year full-time program with at least 120 credits.
- Must include engineering practice training, plus a **year-4** project or design work on complex (for SA, broadly-defined) problems.
- All year levels must be taught, and **at least 2 graduating classes** must have finished.
- **Annual cycle:**
  - SER submitted in week 1 of **May**;
  - screening in June; fee in July;
  - PEV review and requests for more documents in Aug–Oct;
  - **site visit in Nov–Dec**; fact-finding report 1 week after the visit, and the program replies within 1 week;
  - Decision Meeting in March; Council approval and notification in May.
- ⚠️ **For CPE:** the curriculum we are mapping is the **2568** curriculum. Its first class graduates around 2571, and its second around 2572. A 2570 submission must therefore present PO data from the cohorts actually graduating, which is the previous curriculum, or the program gets only *provisional* status. SER item 5-1.1 requires a Table 5.1 for **both** the old and the revised curriculum when the 2 years of data span them. **Needs a decision** (see §8).

## 5. Direct and indirect assessment rules (clarified)
- **Minimums:**
  - PO1–3: at least **2 direct + 1 indirect**.
  - PO4–11: at least **1 direct + 2 indirect**.
- **What "1 direct" means:** one *set* of courses that together assess **all** PIs of that PO. A second direct is another set that covers all the PIs again. Different POs can use different courses for PIs that point to the same SK. This agrees with our per-PI counting in the matrix.
- **PO2 and PO3** must be directly assessed in **≥ 2 courses that really involve complex (SA: broadly-defined) problems**, not ordinary exam questions.
- Show each direct tool in detail (embedded exam items in the SER, or details in the SER with full evidence in the Course Portfolio). A course name alone is not enough.
- **OBA guideline:**
  - Present each PO for the graduating cohort, using **year 3–4 core courses**; lower-year courses may support.
  - Not every PO has to be assessed every year (a 2–3-year rotation is allowed), but at submission all 11 must be shown for 2 years.
  - Sampling is allowed only for large programs.
  - Classroom (formative) assessment should not count toward grades.
- **Indirect:** surveys of students, graduates and alumni, aimed at the POs. This matches decision 10 in our log (Exit Survey and Co-op Employer Survey).
- Required tables, each for 2 consecutive years:
  - (a) the PO assessment plan;
  - (b) a target for each PO;
  - (c) results vs target, plus an improvement plan covering curriculum, teaching, assessment and resources.

## 6. Capstone Design Project rules
- **Problem:**
  - real (or part of a real) situation;
  - needs **≥ 2 sub-disciplines**;
  - the report must show the design process (requirements → problem → research → codes and laws → alternatives → selection).
- **Topic sources:**
  - faculty consulting work;
  - **IAB-assigned** problems;
  - competitions (approved);
  - student proposals (approved);
  - industry (approved).
- **POs:** CLOs should cover **at least PO2, PO3, PO4, PO6, PO7** (capstone guideline). The earlier SA training also required PO8.
- **Management:**
  - a **capstone committee** approves topics and scope, advisor and examiner qualifications, the advising process, the assessment schedule and the annual improvement review;
  - each project has advisors from **≥ 2 related fields**, at least 1 of them program faculty;
  - the exam committee includes the advisor, **another expert faculty member**, and the industry or competition sponsor when there is one.
- **Evidence:**
  - keep work from **every** project, not samples;
  - **Table 1** per project: each PI, weight, group score, **individual student scores**;
  - **Table 2**: all teams, average per PI;
  - show the rubric for every PI, and make the rubric criteria fit the PI content.
- **On site:** PEVs choose **2 projects**. For each, the program presents for about 20 min:
  - how the topic was obtained;
  - a **problem-characteristic checklist table**;
  - the PIs assessed;
  - the design steps, with soft skills and sustainability;
  - the content;
  - all rubrics;
  - the group and individual results;
  - the conclusion.
- **Co-op as capstone (SER guideline 5-1.6):** allowed, **if** the program shows that co-op students are assessed equivalently to non-co-op students, and that industry problems are screened against the problem definition. This is directly relevant to the CPE gap Pre-Project (Y3/S2) → Co-op (Y4/S1) → Project (Y4/S2).

### Complex vs broadly-defined problems (important for SA)
- The PEV decks define **complex** problems with 7 characteristics and require **at least 4 (no. 1 plus 3 others)**. These decks are written for WA.
- The **Manual's glossary** (item 16) defines a **broadly-defined problem** as one solvable through detailed processing and discipline knowledge focused on **applying developed technology**, with 8 characteristics:
  1. varied elements, possibly with conflicting constraints;
  2. solvable by analysis and application of proven engineering technology;
  3. uses the field's principles and practices with existing technology, within a multi-disciplinary working environment;
  4. has accepted solution approaches;
  5. some parts may fall outside codes of practice;
  6. diverse stakeholders;
  7. local consequences that may spread;
  8. part of a complex problem.
- This **8-item list differs from the SP1–SP7 list** in `TABEE_Sydney_Accord_Curriculum_Notes.md` §3 (taken from the ศ.สมศักดิ์ IEA table). None of these documents states a minimum count for SA. **Still unconfirmed.** Ask TABEE or the faculty which list and threshold apply, and use the same list in the capstone checklist table.

## 7. Course Portfolio — required for every course used to assess POs (submitted with the SER)
1. Table of contents.
2. Syllabus showing CLO → PI → PO (with knowledge profile).
3. Teaching materials.
4. Assignments.
5. **Samples of student work or exams at 3 levels (good / average / needs improvement), ≥ 2 per level, marked with the scores given** (names may be removed). For the capstone: all projects.
6. CLO/PI/PO assessment:
   - criteria;
   - pass threshold as a % of students;
   - the exam items or rubric used for each PI;
   - direct and indirect results.
7. Student feedback.
8. Lecturer's reflection and improvement plan, including feedback passed on to related (prerequisite) courses.

PEVs request portfolios **during the SER review** (Aug–Oct), not only on site.

## 8. Site visit (2 days plus the evening before)
- **Day 1:**
  - executives' overview (keep it short; PEVs will cut it off);
  - **program managers on PO assessment and CQI**;
  - labs, library and IT, focused on safety;
  - interviews with **≥ 3 lecturers who may not be committee members**, about their part in curriculum improvement and assessment;
  - interviews with alumni (3) and employers (3), 15 min each; graduates (6); and **students, 6 per year in Y2–Y4** (often online).
- **Day 2:**
  - faculty executives on budget and support;
  - support staff and lab technicians (3);
  - **capstone presentation of 2 projects**;
  - PEVs confirm facts with the program;
  - exit meeting (facts only, no verdict).
- **What interviewees will be asked:**
  - Students and alumni: Do you understand OBE? Do you know the program's PO and PEO? How easy is it to reach your advisor, the labs, IT and the library? Are safety measures adequate? Is there career guidance? What should the program change?
  - Employers: How many of our graduates do you hire? Their strengths and weaknesses? Their language, tool, software and problem-solving skills, and lifelong learning? Do you know our PO and PEO?
  - → **Students, alumni and IAB/employers must actually know the CPE PO and PEO.**

## 9. IAB and stakeholders
- Stakeholders: students, parents, alumni, employers, professional bodies, government, industry.
- IAB:
  - up to 15 members;
  - chaired by an executive or a representative of the field;
  - members from employers, professional bodies, government, industry and alumni;
  - secretary from the program.
- The program meets the IAB **1–2 times a year** to report PO results and propose improvements. IAB members may also supply capstone problems.

### New, concrete rules from the ETAC seminar
1. **One PI matches exactly one SK** of its PO: PO → SK → PI → CLO. Write CLOs to fit the paired PI. Each PI row in the plan carries its SK.
2. **The PO–PI–CLO matrix should list only the courses that are mainly responsible for a PI and actually assess it.** Not every course has to answer every PO. This supports keeping the matrix lean rather than mapping all 294 CLOs.
3. **Assessment-plan table for each PO and each year:**
   - Columns: SK | PI | Direct/Indirect | source (course or survey) | academic year | **expected % of students passing the PI** (the example uses 70, or 60).
   - Example for PO1: each PI has 2 courses + the **Student Exit Survey**, repeated for **2568** and **2569**.
4. **PI achievement calculation (worked example):**
   - PI1.3 (2568) = 0.45 × 56% (Engineering Economy CLO1) + 0.45 × 66% (PPC CLO2) + 0.10 × 70% (exit survey) = **61.9% vs a 60% target, so achieved**.
   - **Rule: the indirect weights combined must not exceed ½ of the weight of one direct assessment.**
   - → The workbook's "attainment %" column on the PO1–PO11 sheets needs these weights and the formula. Today it is a plain input.
5. **CLO achievement charts per course** (% achieved vs % not achieved vs target, for each CLO→PI→PO→PLO) appear in the Course Portfolio. A "solution & marking scheme" shows which exam item measures which PI/CLO.
6. **Three-level CQI:**
   - Level 1, lecturer: the course portfolio plus an improvement plan.
   - Level 2, program committee: overall analysis and structural or course changes.
   - Level 3, IAB/IAC: structural problems go to the IAB/IAC.
   - Report shape: PO/PI result vs target → lecturer's improvement → committee's improvement.
7. **Documents PEVs actually requested (seminar examples):**
   - advising records for students with **GPA < 2.00**, for 2 years (2567–2568);
   - **satisfaction surveys** on extracurricular activities and career guidance, with the **number of respondents vs participants**;
   - **IAB minutes 1/2567 and 1/2568** on PEO and PLO changes, with evidence that **each year's PO results were used** in the PEO review;
   - how the program ensures that courses **transferred in from other institutions** and used for PO assessment are of equivalent standard.
8. **Criterion 1 additions (ETAC template):**
   - number of graduates for **≥ 2 years**;
   - progress monitoring, especially of **prerequisite** chains;
   - a summary of graduation-approval criteria and the documents checked;
   - **anonymised sample transcripts** for every option, sent separately from the SER, with an explanation of how to read them.
9. **AUN-QA cross-references** sit next to each criterion (e.g. 1.1, 1.4, 2.3, 2.7, 4.7, 6.1–6.4, 8.1). One evidence set can serve both.
